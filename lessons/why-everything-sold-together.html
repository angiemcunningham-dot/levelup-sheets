Why Everything Sold Together, and Why Sunday Night Looked Different

Last week stocks, gold, silver, and crypto all fell at the same time. That is rare, and it has a name. This lesson walks through what happened, the one idea that explains it, the four gauges professionals use to tell a flush from a crash, why Sunday's reopening leaned bullish, and the honest answer to whether that is a short-term bounce or a long-term bottom.

Reading time about 12 minutes · Updated Monday morning with what the market actually did · Education, not advice
Lesson 1

What actually happened this week

The S&P 500 fell 8.6% in five days, its worst week since the tariff panic of April 2025. The Nasdaq 100 fell 10%. Nvidia, the biggest company in the AI trade, lost 23% in a week after news that regulators are looking at its accounting. Bitcoin fell 14% and touched its lowest price since September 2024.

Here is the part that matters for the lesson. Gold fell 9.5%. Silver fell 16%. Copper fell 10%. The only thing that went up was the U.S. dollar.

Why that is strange

Gold is supposed to go up when people are scared. It did not. When the "safe" things fall with the "risky" things, fear is not the whole story. Something else is going on.

Lesson 2

The big idea: forced selling is not the same as fear

Imagine a family that needs rent money by Friday. They do not sell their worst stuff. They sell whatever sells fastest: the TV, the bike, the gold ring. The sale says nothing about whether the bike was a good bike. It says they needed cash.

Big funds work the same way. Many of them borrow to invest, and many are required by their own rules to cut risk when prices swing hard. When the AI stocks broke, those funds had to raise cash immediately, and they sold what they could: gold, silver, crypto, anything with a buyer. That is why everything fell together. The dollar rose because cash is what everyone was reaching for.

A fear-driven selloff looks like

  • Stocks down, gold up, bonds up
  • People paying a lot for insurance (options)
  • Lenders pulling back; credit gets expensive
  • Most stocks hitting new lows, not just the leaders

A forced-cash selloff looks like

  • Stocks down, gold down, crypto down, dollar up
  • Insurance cost rising mostly because prices moved, not because people are buying more of it
  • Lenders cautious but still lending
  • Damage concentrated in the crowded trade
Why it matters

Forced selling ends when the selling is done, not when the news gets better. That is why these episodes often bounce hard before the headlines improve. It also means the bounce proves nothing about the long term. It only proves the sellers ran out.

Lesson 3

Four gauges that tell a flush from a crash

You do not need a Bloomberg terminal. These four readings are public, and together they answer the only question that matters this week: is the selling exhausting itself, or is something breaking?

Peak fear
1. The fear gauge and its curve (VIX)The VIX measures how much people are paying for stock-market insurance. Friday it closed at 35.9, the highest since April 2025. The useful detail is the curve: the 9-day VIX (40.7) is above the 1-month (35.9), which is above the 3-month (30.9). Translation: the market is more afraid of the next nine days than of the next three months. That shape only appears at the peak of a panic. In the ten previous first-time readings like this since 2007, stocks were higher five days later nine times out of ten.
Noticed, not broken
2. Credit: are the lenders panicking?When companies borrow, they pay a premium over government rates. That premium widened by 0.32% this week to about 3.5%. In March 2020 it went above 10%. In April 2025 it reached 4.6%. Lenders noticed, and they are charging more. They have not stopped lending. If this number jumps again Monday while stocks bounce, the lesson changes.
Concentrated
3. Breadth: how many stocks are actually broken?Of 501 large U.S. stocks, only 26 hit a new one-year low on Friday. That is 5%. In a true crash a third of them do. The index fell hard because its largest names fell hard, not because the average company is in trouble. The equal-weighted S&P fell 6.2% while the regular one fell 8.6%.
Bounced
4. The weekend proxy: crypto never closesStocks sleep from Friday 4 PM to Sunday 6 PM. Crypto does not, so it shows what sellers do when nothing else is open. Bitcoin fell to 55,181 at 1 AM Saturday, then bounced 10% and climbed back above its Friday 4 PM level by Sunday evening. Stock futures then opened Sunday night up 1.3%.

The fear curve on Friday

Normal shape slopes up to the right. Friday's sloped down: near-term fear above long-term fear. That is what capitulation looks like on a chart.

Lesson 4

Why Sunday night leaned bullish

Put the four gauges together and add what happened at the reopening:

  • Peak-fear shape. The fear curve inverted, which historically marks the worst of the selling more often than the start of it.
  • Credit held. Lenders got cautious but did not shut the door. That separates this from 2008 and March 2020, the two times a gold-and-stocks selloff like this one went on to get much worse.
  • The damage is narrow. Most stocks are bruised, not broken. The pain is concentrated in AI and chips.
  • The weekend sellers were met. Bitcoin's Saturday low was bought, and it reclaimed Friday's level. Ether bounced 12%. When the only open market stops falling, the sellers have likely run out for now.
  • Stocks opened higher. S&P futures opened Sunday 125 points above Friday's low. Gold caught a bid at the same time, and oil faded. That combination is the opposite of Friday's forced-selling pattern.
  • The machines are mostly done. Goldman's trading desk estimates trend-following funds have already sold 57 billion dollars of stock this month and are now net short. Their remaining selling is small if the market stabilizes. Funds that are short have to buy to get out, and that is what fuels a sharp bounce.
  • History's base rate. Since 1990, the first time the VIX closes above 35 after a calm stretch, the S&P has been higher three months later 78% of the time, with a median gain of about 10%.
In one sentence

The evidence says this was a flush, not a break, and flushes tend to bounce. That is a statement about the next few weeks, not the next year.

Lesson 5

Short term or long term? The honest answer is both, with different confidence.

Higher confidence Short term: days to three weeks

  • The forced sellers are mostly done and the short sellers have to cover.
  • Fear peaked on Friday by the shape of the curve.
  • Thanksgiving week is historically one of the calmer, more positive weeks of the year.
  • At the end of November, pension funds must buy stocks to rebalance after a month this bad. That is scheduled buying.

Unproven Long term: months

  • The dry powder is shrinking. The total supply of stablecoins, the cash that sits inside crypto waiting to buy, is down 6% in a month and 10% from its May peak. Crypto cannot start a lasting uptrend while the money to buy it is leaving. This is the single most important long-term gauge in this lesson.
  • Big money is still leaving. Bitcoin ETFs have seen 3.5 billion dollars of outflows in November. A lasting bottom needs that to turn.
  • The Fed is leaning toward raising rates on December 9. Rate hikes into a selloff are a headwind, not a tailwind.
  • The Nvidia question is not answered. If the accounting probe turns into a restatement, the AI trade has another leg down and everything else gets dragged again.
How to hold both ideas at once

A bounce can be real and still be temporary. Professionals trade the short term with small size and tight rules, and they wait for the long-term gauges to turn before they call it a bottom. The long-term gauges have not turned. They may, and this lesson will tell you what turning looks like.

Lesson 6

Three things to watch Monday, in plain words

  1. Does Bitcoin stay above Friday's level? That level is 58,900. Above it, the weekend bounce is holding. Below it, the sellers came back and the lesson resets.
  2. Does fear calm down? Watch for the VIX to fall from the mid-30s, and for the 9-day VIX to drop back under the 1-month. That is the market saying the next week is no longer scarier than the next quarter. In December 2018, August 2024 and April 2025 that happened within two days of the low.
  3. Do the lenders stay calm? If the bond ETF called HYG falls more than 1.5% while stocks rise, credit is voting against the bounce. That is the one signal that would turn this lesson bearish, no matter how good the stock chart looks.

Two more for the curious: U.S. stock markets reopen 9:30 AM ET Monday, Japan is closed for a holiday, and the week's big inflation report comes Wednesday morning before the Thanksgiving break.

Lesson 7

What this means for how you behave, not what you buy

  • Do not chase a Sunday night gap. The first hour after a week like this is information, not an entry. Let the level hold first.
  • The level is the line in the sand. Chelsea teaches that a level is only real once price tests it and holds. Friday's low in stocks and Friday's 4 PM level in Bitcoin are this week's lines. Everything above them is a bounce. A close below them cancels the thesis.
  • Size for the bad case, not the good one. After the two worst versions of this setup, 2008 and 2020, stocks fell another 26% to 36% before bottoming. No one is predicting that. Everyone should be able to survive it.
  • Crypto size stays small until the dry powder returns. Shrinking stablecoin supply means thin order books and wicky charts. That is when stops get hunted. Smaller size, wider stops, fewer trades.
  • Write down what would make you wrong before you act. For this lesson it is written down in Lesson 6. Do the same for your own trades.
Lesson 8 · Added Monday

What Monday actually did, and what it teaches

Before the open, Nvidia's board reversed its decision from a week earlier and brought Jensen Huang back as CEO. The stock jumped more than 30% at the open. Chip stocks had their best day since April 2025. The S&P 500 rose over 5% in the first hour, its biggest gain since the April 2025 tariff reversal. Bitcoin climbed above 63,000.

Lesson 6's three watch items, graded

Three things this teaches

  • Structure sets the size, news sets the timing. Nobody could have predicted Huang's return on Sunday night. What was predictable was that the market was coiled: fear had peaked, the big funds had already sold, and anyone short had to buy to get out. Good news landing on a coiled spring produces a 5% day. The same news on a calm market produces a 1% day.
  • The bounce goes to what was sold hardest, not what held best. The "safe" stocks that held their levels last week barely moved today. The chip stocks that were crushed gained 15% to 35%. When forced sellers reverse, they buy back what they sold. That is why chasing "strong" names after a flush often underperforms.
  • A gap is information, not an entry. Buying Nvidia in the first hour meant paying 170 for a stock that closed Friday at 125. Lesson 7 said do not chase a gap; the gap has to hold into the close first. That rule exists for days exactly like this one.

Is it a long-term bottom now?

  • The short-term call played out faster and bigger than expected. Short term, the lesson was right.
  • The long-term gauges have not moved yet. Stablecoin supply is still shrinking. Bitcoin ETF flows for today are not published until tonight. The Fed is still expected to raise rates on December 9. And Huang's return answers who runs Nvidia, not the SEC's question about Nvidia's accounting.
  • What would upgrade it. Stablecoin supply growing for a week, ETF inflows for three straight days, the VIX back under 20, and Bitcoin holding above its Friday level through the week. Watch those four; they are the difference between a relief rally and a new trend.
For the group

If you felt sick on Friday and euphoric on Monday, that is the emotional whipsaw forced selling is designed to produce. The gauges in Lesson 3 did not feel either of those things. They said "flush, likely to bounce, long term unproven" on Saturday, and they say the same thing today with the bounce now booked. Learning to read the gauges instead of your stomach is the whole point of this lesson.

Check yourself

Three questions, then a glossary

Why did gold fall during a scary week when gold is supposed to be "safe"?
Because this was a forced-cash selloff, not a fear selloff. Funds that needed cash sold whatever would sell, including gold. The dollar was the only thing that rose because cash was what everyone wanted.
Why does it matter that only 26 of 501 big stocks hit new lows?
It shows the damage is concentrated in one crowded trade, the AI and chip names, rather than spread through the whole economy. In a real crash a third of stocks make new lows. Narrow damage usually heals faster.
If Sunday night was bullish, why isn't this a long-term bottom?
Because the long-term gauges have not turned: stablecoin supply is still shrinking, Bitcoin ETFs are still seeing outflows, the Fed is still leaning toward a rate hike, and the Nvidia question is unresolved. A bounce can be real and temporary at the same time.

Glossary

VIX
The "fear gauge." It measures the price of insurance on the S&P 500. Higher means people are paying more to protect themselves.
VIX curve
The VIX measured over different time windows (9 days, 1 month, 3 months). When short windows are higher than long ones, fear is concentrated in the immediate future, which usually means panic is peaking.
Futures
Contracts that let people trade the stock market when the exchange is closed. They reopen Sunday at 6 PM ET and are the first read on Monday.
Credit spread
The extra interest companies pay to borrow compared with the government. Wider spreads mean lenders are nervous.
Breadth
How many stocks are participating in a move. Narrow breadth means a few giants are driving the index.
Forced selling
Selling that happens because a rule or a margin call requires it, not because the seller changed their mind about the asset.
Liquidation
In crypto, when a leveraged position is closed automatically because the price moved against it. Billions of dollars were liquidated this week, mostly longs.
Stablecoin supply
The total amount of dollar-pegged coins (USDT, USDC and others) in existence. It is the cash sitting inside crypto. When it shrinks, there is less money available to buy.
ETF flows
Money moving into or out of funds that hold Bitcoin on behalf of regular investors. A clean read on what institutions are doing.
Line in the sand
A price level that institutions are visibly defending. It is either held or it is not, and the answer changes the plan.
